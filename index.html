The question

Most bugs I have chased did not live inside a component. They lived between components, where one part's ideal assumptions meet another part's real behavior.

So the question behind all of my work is the same: does what holds under ideal conditions still hold under real ones? Between a simulator and a robot, between an offline benchmark and a live system, between a desktop GPU and a phone.

Autonomous mower · 2025–2026

A day on the grass

A remote-controlled mower, a production ROS stack, and a team new to both. The robot drove diagonally while every sensor looked fine; the fault was between them. The next year I turned that lesson into calibration, safety, and planning tools, still on real hardware only. That is why I build digital twins.

Simulation-ready scenes

A world a robot can use

3D reconstructions of real rooms look right but are too heavy and too bumpy to drive on. I turn them into lightweight scenes a mobile robot can collide against in simulation.

  • Geometry processing
  • 3D Gaussian Splatting

02

SimRosetta

One task, three simulators

Like the Rosetta Stone, which carries one text in three scripts, SimRosetta carries one robot task into several physics simulators, and uses their disagreement to train sturdier policies.

  • Built on open-source RoboVerse
  • Reinforcement learning

Three days on site

Full-stack integration of a competition robot for navigation, object retrieval, and bridge crossing. Moving from simulation to the real car, only the high-level logic survived.

TwinNudge

The gap you cannot see

The same command puts a real arm and its twin in slightly different poses, too small to see and large enough to fail a grasp. TwinNudge aligns the twin from camera images.

Geometry · State · Behavior

A twin you can trust

Three of these chapters each took on one layer of a digital twin: the world a robot moves through, how physics responds, and where the robot really is. In graduate school I want to join the three into one twin, and test whether policies trained on it, aligned to the real robot and across several simulators, hold up better on real hardware.
